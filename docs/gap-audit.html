The one sentence version

The site already computes more than Pikalytics does. What it doesn't do is let you act on any of it. Almost every number on screen is dead text — you can read that Sucker Punch is on 99.5% of Kingambit, but you then have to go and find Sucker Punch yourself and tick it. That single missing behaviour — click a statistic, get the thing — accounts for the largest share of the friction, and it's also the cheapest category to fix.

One caveat on the data ceiling. Pikalytics publishes usage, win rate, moves, items, abilities and teammates for exactly 50 Pokémon in Regulation M-B, and publishes no EV spreads at all. Several things below ("show the most-used spread", "rank all 600") are capped by what the source gives us, not by our code. Where that's the case, I've said so rather than promising a feature we can't feed.

Grouped by theme, each tagged by how much it stands between you and a finished team. BLOCKING means you're actively fighting the interface; MAJOR means a capability a rival site has and we don't; POLISH is friction; LATER belongs to a future phase of the original plan.

Proposed build order

Ordered by how much each tier moves the site toward "most convenient website ever", against what it costs to build. Tiers 1 and 2 are mostly wiring work over data and endpoints that already exist, which is why they come first — the biggest gains are also the cheapest.

Tier 1

Make every number clickable

Your central complaint, and the single change that most alters how the site feels to use. Almost no new data required — it's turning existing displays into controls.

  1. Click any usage entry to apply it. Item, move, ability and teammate rows become buttons that write straight into the open slot. SlotEditor.tsx · UsageSection
  2. "Use most-used set" button on every slot, plus auto-apply when you add a tracked Pokémon — fills the top item, top ability and top four moves in one action. new setBuilder.ts + SlotEditor
  3. Add-to-team buttons everywhere — Pokédex rows, the Pokémon page, Checks & Counters rows, and Move IQ suggestions. PokemonTable · PokemonDetail · MoveIQPanel
  4. Import a Top Team or a Team Core into a new team in one click. TopTeamsPanel · CoresPanel
Tier 2

Show the data we already have

Pure display work against fields already in the database and already served by the API. Highest information gain per hour of anything on this list.

  1. Item and ability usage on the Pokémon page, laid out like the existing moves list. PokemonDetail
  2. Usage % in the rankings, alongside win rate and record; round win rates to one decimal. MetaRankings
  3. Rising / falling indicator per Pokémon, from the orphaned trend endpoint. GET /api/meta/trend
  4. "Meta data as of ⟨date⟩" stamp in the header. UsageSnapshot table
Tier 3

Search everything, reach everything

Directly answers "I want to search my abilities", and lifts the cap that currently hides 93% of the imported dex.

  1. Multi-field Pokémon search — ability, type, stat thresholds and role, in the same box, Showdown-style. routers/pokemon.py · list_pokemon
  2. Raise the limit and add paging so the full 1,351 dex is genuinely browsable. le=500 cap
  3. Type filter chips and a usage-% sort on the Pokédex table.
  4. Standalone move, ability and item browsers as their own pages, with "which Pokémon learn this" reverse lookup.
Tier 4

Answer "what beats my team?"

The analytical heart of what you described. The matchup matrix built for Breaker/Waller already computes the raw numbers — this is a new reading of them plus targeted additions.

  1. Team Threat Report — score every top-25 Pokémon against all six of yours at once and rank by total pressure, so the Pokémon nobody on your team handles rises to the top. extends /api/calc/team-matchups
  2. Load from team in the damage calculator, both sides. DamageCalculator
  3. Meta Calcs opens on a top-25 grid instead of a blank search box.
  4. Offensive coverage grid beside the defensive one, and factor immunity abilities into both. typeChart.ts
  5. Speed ladder interleaving your team with the meta's speeds, with Tailwind / Scarf / paralysis toggles.
  6. Role-based EV presets for meta targets, replacing the current neutral-EV assumption, clearly labelled as an assumption.
  7. Score your team against the 10 real tournament teams.
Tier 5

The beginner on-ramp

You asked for this to work without explanations for now — so it's about surfacing good defaults, not writing tutorials.

  1. A "Start here" home page — current best Pokémon, best sets, and three importable winning teams.
  2. Recommended set generator — one button gives a complete competitive set for any of the 50 tracked Pokémon.
  3. Role labels (physical attacker, wall, speed control, support) derived from base stats and the most-used moveset.
  4. Starter teams ready to import and modify.
Tier 6

Convenience and polish

Individually small, collectively the difference between "works" and "nice to use".

  1. Loading skeletons and real error states on every panel.
  2. Whole team card clickable; rename and folder creation from the list.
  3. Export teams to Showdown text, closing the round trip.
  4. Keyboard navigation in every search dropdown.
  5. Duplicate-item, duplicate-species and EV-overspend warnings.
  6. Promote the meta pages to top-level navigation; make teams URL-addressable.
  7. Mobile layout.
Tier 7

Later phases

Unchanged from the original plan, and genuinely lower priority than everything above.

  1. Trend charts over accumulated snapshots (Phase 5).
  2. Tournament browser and results pages (Phase 2).
  3. Spreadsheet ingestion (Phase 3).
  4. Practice and game-log tracking (Phase 4).
  5. Deployment, so the site outlives your dev servers.

Suggested first move. Tier 1 plus item 5 of Tier 2 is a single coherent session and would change the day-to-day feel of the site more than everything below it combined — after it, every statistic on screen becomes a control, and the Pokémon page finally shows the item and ability data we've had all along.
